## 2. Mathematical Foundations

### 2.1 The Kadomtsev-Petviashvili II Equation
The KP-II equation governs non-linear wave evolution in weakly dispersive media with transverse effects:
$$\partial_x \left( \partial_t u + 6 u \partial_x u + \partial_x^3 u \right) + 3 \partial_y^2 u = 0$$
Integrating once with respect to $x$ under decaying boundary conditions yields the dynamical flow:
$$\partial_t u = -6 u \partial_x u - \partial_x^3 u - 3 \partial_x^{-1} \partial_y^2 u$$
In localized coordinate patches, the transverse contribution is stabilized via the second-order transverse Laplacian $\frac{1}{2} \partial_y^2 u$.

### 2.2 The Sato Grassmannian & Tau-Functions
In the algebraic theory of Sato, every $N$-soliton solution to the KP hierarchy is generated by a $\tau$-function defined by a Wronskian determinant:
$$\tau(x, y, t) = \operatorname{Wr}(f_1, f_2, \dots, f_N)$$
where each $f_i(x, y, t) = \sum_{j=1}^M A_{ij} \exp(\theta_j)$ with phase $\theta_j = k_j x + k_j^2 y + k_j^3 t$. The physical solution $u(x, y, t)$ is reconstructed via the logarithmic derivative:
$$u(x, y, t) = 2 \partial_x^2 \ln \tau(x, y, t)$$
The non-degeneracy of $u$ is guaranteed by the totally non-negative Grassmannian $\mathrm{Gr}_{\ge 0}(N, M)$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

### 2.1 The Hyperbolic Calogero-Moser Hamiltonian
For $N$ identical particles on the real line with coordinates $q = (q_1, \dots, q_N)$ and conjugate momenta $p = (p_1, \dots, p_N)$, the hyperbolic Calogero-Moser Hamiltonian is:
$$\mathcal{H}(q, p) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^N p_i^2 + g^2 \sum_{1 \le i < j \le N} \frac{1}{\sinh^2(q_i - q_j)}$$
The resulting equations of motion are:
$$\dot{q}_i = p_i, \quad \dot{p}_i = 2 g^2 \sum_{j \ne i} \frac{\cosh(q_i - q_j)}{\sinh^3(q_i - q_j)} - \gamma p_i$$

### 2.2 Lax Pair Formulation & Dunkl Differential Operators
Integrability is manifested via the $N \times N$ Lax matrix $L$ and skew-symmetric matrix $M$:
$$L_{ij} = p_i \delta_{ij} + (1 - \delta_{ij}) \frac{i g}{\sinh(q_i - q_j)}$$
$$M_{ij} = \delta_{ij} \left( g \sum_{k \ne i} \frac{\cosh(q_i - q_k)}{\sinh^2(q_i - q_k)} \right) - (1 - \delta_{ij}) \frac{g \cosh(q_i - q_j)}{\sinh^2(q_i - q_j)}$$
The Hamiltonian flow satisfies the Lax equation $\dot{L} = [L, M]$, ensuring that the spectrum of $L$ is strictly time-invariant. Consequently, the trace functions $I_k = \frac{1}{k} \operatorname{Tr}(L^k)$ form $N$ Poisson-commuting constants of motion:
$$\{I_k, I_m\} = 0 \quad \forall \, k, m \in \{1, \dots, N\}$$

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

### 2.1 The Benjamin-Ono Integro-Differential Equation
The Benjamin-Ono equation for a real-valued scalar wave $u: \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is:
$$\partial_t u + u \partial_x u + \mathcal{H}(\partial_x^2 u) = 0$$
where $\mathcal{H}$ denotes the principal value Hilbert transform:
$$\mathcal{H}(f)(x) = \frac{1}{\pi} \text{P.V.} \int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{f(y)}{x - y} \, dy$$
On a periodic boundary domain $x \in [0, 2\pi)$, the continuous Hilbert kernel is replaced by the periodic cotangent convolution operator:
$$\mathcal{H}_{\text{per}}(f)(x_i) = \frac{1}{2N} \sum_{j \ne i} f(x_j) \cot\left( \frac{x_i - x_j}{2} \right)$$

### 2.2 Complex Pole Dynamics
The $N$-soliton solution to the Benjamin-Ono equation can be parameterized by $N$ complex conjugate pole pairs in the complex plane:
$$u(x, t) = \sum_{j=1}^N \frac{2 i}{x - x_j(t)} - \sum_{j=1}^N \frac{2 i}{x - \bar{x}_j(t)}$$
where $x_j(t) = \xi_j(t) + i \eta_j(t)$ with $\eta_j(t) > 0$. The poles evolve according to Calogero-Moser-type equations of motion:
$$\dot{x}_j = -i \sum_{k \ne j} \frac{2}{x_j - x_k}$$
guaranteeing complete integrability and the conservation of an infinite hierarchy of polynomial action invariants $I_n = \int_{-\infty}^\infty u^n dx$.

### 2.2 Discrete Multi-Peakon Dynamics

$$\dot{q}_i = \frac{\partial \mathcal{H}}{\partial p_i} = \sum_{j=1}^N p_j e^{-|q_i - q_j|}$$
$$\dot{p}_i = -\frac{\partial \mathcal{H}}{\partial q_i} = \sum_{j=1}^N p_i p_j \operatorname{sgn}(q_i - q_j) e^{-|q_i - q_j|} - \gamma p_i$$
