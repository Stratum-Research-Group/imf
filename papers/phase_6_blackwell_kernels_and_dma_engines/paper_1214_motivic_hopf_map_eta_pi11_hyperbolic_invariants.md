## Abstract
This paper analyzes the geometric construction and topological consequences of the motivic Hopf map $\eta \in \pi_{1, 1}(\mathbf{1})$. Originating from the geometric projection $\mathbb{A}^2 \setminus \{0\} \to \mathbb{P}^1$, the motivic Hopf map encodes quadratic signatures and governs the non-torsion phenomena of motivic homotopy theory.

## 1. Geometric Construction of eta
The algebraic Hopf map is the projection morphism:
$$\eta: \mathbb{A}^2 \setminus \{0\} \longrightarrow \mathbb{P}^1, \quad (x, y) \longmapsto [x : y]$$
In motivic homotopy theory, $\mathbb{A}^2 \setminus \{0\} \simeq S^{3, 2}$ and $\mathbb{P}^1 \simeq S^{2, 1}$. Suspending yields the stable element:
$$\eta \in \pi_{1, 1}(\mathbf{1})$$
Under the real topological realization functor $k \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}$, $\eta$ maps to the classical Hopf map $S^3 \to S^2$.
