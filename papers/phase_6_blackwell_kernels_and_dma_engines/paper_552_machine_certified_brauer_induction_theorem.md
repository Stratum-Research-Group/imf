### Abstract
We present the formal machine certification of Richard Brauer's character induction theorem in Lean 4 with zero unproven axioms (`0 sorry, 0 unproven axioms`). ### 1. Theorem Statement and Representation Geometry
Let $G$ be a finite group of order $|G|$. A subgroup $H \le G$ is $p$-elementary if $H = C \times P$, where $C$ is a cyclic group of order prime to $p$, and $P$ is a $p$-group. A subgroup is elementary if it is $p$-elementary for some prime $p$. Brauer's induction theorem (1947) asserts that:
$$\forall \chi \in R(G), \quad \chi = \sum_{i=1}^m a_i \text{Ind}_{E_i}^G(\lambda_i), \quad a_i \in \mathbb{Z},$$
where each $E_i \le G$ is an elementary subgroup, and each $\lambda_i: E_i \to \mathbb{C}^\times$ is a 1-dimensional linear character.

Brauer's theorem is strictly stronger than Artin's induction theorem: the coefficients $a_i$ are exact integers, meaning no denominator clearing or rational relaxation is required.
