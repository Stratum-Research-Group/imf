### 1. Theorem Statement and Rational Invariants
Let $G$ be a finite group of order $|G|$. A subgroup $C \le G$ is cyclic if it is generated by a single element $g \in G$. Artin's induction theorem (1923) asserts that:
$$\forall \chi \in R(G), \quad |G| \chi = \sum_{i=1}^m c_i \text{Ind}_{C_i}^G(\lambda_i), \quad c_i \in \mathbb{Z},$$
where each $C_i \le G$ is a cyclic subgroup and $\lambda_i: C_i \to \mathbb{C}^\times$ is a 1-dimensional character.

Equivalently, the map:
$$\bigoplus_{C \in \text{Cyclic}(G)} R(C) \otimes \mathbb{Q} \xrightarrow{\text{Ind}} R(G) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$$
is surjective, demonstrating that over $\mathbb{Q}$, all representation theory of finite groups reduces to abelian Fourier analysis over cyclic subgroups.
