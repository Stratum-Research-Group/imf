## Abstract
We investigate the foundations of non-archimedean rigid analytic geometry in the sense of Tate and Coleman. Over a $p$-adic field $\mathbb{Q}_p$, the topology generated by open balls is totally disconnected, rendering naive line integrals path-independent only locally. Robert Coleman solved this foundational challenge by constructing a unique antiderivative functor on overconvergent differential forms satisfying Frobenius equivariance.

### 1. Coleman Antiderivatives and Wide Open Discs

Let $X$ be a smooth projective curve over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with good reduction over $\mathbb{F}_p$. A residue disc $D_P \subset X(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ corresponding to $\tilde{P} \in X(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is isomorphic to the rigid analytic open unit disc $\mathbb{B}^1(0, 1^-)$. Within $D_P$, any differential $\omega \in \Omega^1(X)$ expands as a power series:
$$\omega = \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n t^n dt$$
The local Coleman integral is given explicitly by formal antidifferentiation:
$$\int_0^x \omega = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{a_n}{n+1} x^{n+1}$$
which converges for all $x \in p\mathbb{Z}_p$. Coleman extended this integration globally across residue discs by imposing Frobenius equivariance:
$$\int_{\phi(P)}^{\phi(Q)} \omega = \int_P^Q \phi^*(\omega)$$
uniquely pinning down integration constants.
